Balance-sheet tells

Eight inconsistencies that show up before management knows you've found them.

The accounting identity

Assets = Liabilities + Equity. If they don't, the deck is wrong before the story begins. The Balance-sheet checker verifies this and seven more checks.

Eight tells

  1. AR / revenue ratio creeping up. Days sales outstanding rising 30%+ year-over-year — either revenue is being pulled forward or customers are stretching payment because the product isn't sticky enough to enforce terms.
  2. Inventory growing faster than COGS. Either the channel is stuffed or obsolescence is coming.
  3. Capitalised software ballooning. Too much R&D is being capitalised, deferring the expense. Compare cap-software / total R&D to peer norms.
  4. Goodwill > tangible book. A business that's mostly acquisitions, not organic growth.
  5. Deferred revenue collapsing. Bookings have stalled even if revenue (which lags) still looks fine.
  6. Related-party debt. Loans from a founder or affiliate at non-market rates — often a sign of liquidity pressure.
  7. Cash + short-term debt mismatched with reported runway. If runway claims don't reconcile to (cash − non-rolling debt) / monthly burn, there's a hidden assumption.
  8. Negative working capital that's worsening. Negative WC is fine if it's structural (subscription, prepayment); a problem if AR is rising faster than AP.

None of these are conclusive on their own. Two together is a question for management. Three is a reason to slow the timeline.
